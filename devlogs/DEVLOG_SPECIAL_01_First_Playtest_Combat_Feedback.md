# SPECIAL DEVLOG #1 — First Playtest Combat Feedback

This special devlog covers the very first internal playtest of **Obsidian Protocol — Autonomous Warfare**. Unlike the mainline devlogs, which focus on systems and vision, this one is all about raw feedback, unexpected behaviors, emergent moments, and the lessons learned from watching real players interact with autonomous warfare.

---

## 🎮 Playtest Overview

The playtest included:

- 12 internal testers  
- 1 prototype skirmish map  
- 2 early factions  
- Autonomous squads enabled  
- Siege & breach systems active  
- Dynamic battlefield interactions  

The goal wasn’t balance — it was **behavior**.

We wanted to see how players reacted to autonomy, how squads responded to intent, and how the battlefield evolved under real decision‑making.

---

## 🧠 Squad Behavior Feedback

Players consistently noted:

### **1. “They feel alive.”**
Testers were surprised at how squads:

- repositioned under fire  
- used cover intelligently  
- flanked without orders  
- retreated when overwhelmed  
- coordinated with nearby squads  

The autonomy system produced moments that felt scripted — but weren’t.

### **2. “I stopped micromanaging.”**
Players naturally shifted from micro to macro:

- issuing intent zones  
- focusing on timing  
- managing territory  
- coordinating multi‑front pushes  

The game encouraged strategic thinking without forcing it.

### **3. “They surprised me.”**
Some emergent behaviors:

- breach teams choosing alternate walls  
- support squads suppressing enemies before players noticed them  
- recon drones revealing flanking routes players didn’t plan  
- squads forming temporary firing lines  

These moments created genuine excitement.

---

## ⚔️ Combat Flow Feedback

### **1. Breach sequences were a highlight.**
Players loved:

- charge placement  
- detonation timing  
- the push‑through moment  
- interior clearing  
- holding the breach point  

It felt tactical and cinematic.

### **2. Suppression felt powerful.**
Testers noted:

- clear visual feedback  
- meaningful impact on enemy movement  
- strong synergy with flanking units  

Suppression became a core part of their strategy.

### **3. Elevation mattered.**
High ground advantages were immediately noticeable:

- better visibility  
- stronger firing angles  
- safer positioning  

Players started fighting for elevation control early.

---

## 🌑 Battlefield Interaction Feedback

Players reacted strongly to:

- destructible cover  
- breachable structures  
- fog‑of‑war shifts  
- dynamic lighting changes  
- debris affecting movement  

The battlefield felt like a living system, not a static map.

---

## 🔧 Issues & Improvements

Playtesters identified several areas for refinement:

- squads occasionally over‑committed to flanks  
- breach teams sometimes chose suboptimal walls  
- suppression feedback needs more clarity  
- UI intent indicators need stronger visibility  
- heavy units felt slightly too slow in tight spaces  

These issues are already being addressed.

---

## 🔥 Emergent Moments (Player Highlights)

A few standout moments from testers:

- A breach team detonated a wall, only for a support squad to immediately suppress defenders inside — completely unplanned synergy.  
- A recon drone spotted an enemy flank, causing two squads to reposition and intercept without any player micro.  
- A heavy assault unit pushed through debris, creating new cover that another squad used seconds later.  
- An enemy commander shifted doctrine mid‑battle, surprising players with a sudden siege push.  

These moments define the identity of Obsidian Protocol.
