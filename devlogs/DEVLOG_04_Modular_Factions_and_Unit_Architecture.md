# DEVLOG #4 — Modular Factions & Unit Architecture

Welcome back to the development journey of **Obsidian Protocol — Autonomous Warfare**.

Today’s devlog focuses on one of the most important pillars of the project: **modularity**. Obsidian Protocol is designed not just as a game, but as a platform — a foundation for creating factions, units, abilities, tech trees, and entire campaigns without touching core code.

---

## 🏗️ Modular Faction System

Factions in Obsidian Protocol are built from isolated, interchangeable components:

- Units  
- Abilities  
- Structures  
- Tech trees  
- AI personalities  
- Visual identity  

Each component is defined in a clean, structured format. This allows you to:

- Build new factions from scratch  
- Create total conversion mods  
- Experiment with unique unit types  
- Add custom AI doctrines  
- Design full campaigns  
- Iterate rapidly without breaking anything  

Modularity isn’t a convenience — it’s the backbone of the project.

---

## 🔧 Unit Architecture

Units are constructed from modular building blocks:

- **Behavior modules** — movement, targeting, decision‑making  
- **Ability modules** — active and passive skills  
- **Weapon modules** — ballistic, energy, explosive  
- **Movement profiles** — infantry, vehicle, drone  
- **AI personalities** — aggressive, cautious, tactical  
- **Visual skins** — models, materials, animations  

This architecture allows units to be assembled like LEGO pieces. You can swap behaviors, abilities, weapons, or personalities without rewriting logic.

---

## 🎨 Why Modularity Matters

Modularity enables:

- Faster development  
- Cleaner architecture  
- Easier debugging  
- Long‑term scalability  
- Community‑driven content  
- Endless replayability  

Obsidian Protocol is built to grow — not just by me, but by anyone who wants to create.
