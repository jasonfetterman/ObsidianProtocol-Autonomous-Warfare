# DEVLOG #2 — Inside the Mind of a Squad

Welcome back to the development journey of **Obsidian Protocol — Autonomous Warfare**.

In the last devlog, we talked about *why* this project exists — the frustration with static RTS battlefields and units that wait for orders. Today, we’re diving into the core of what makes Obsidian Protocol different:

**Autonomous Squad Intelligence.**

Most RTS units follow scripts.  
They don’t evaluate.  
They don’t adapt.  
They don’t think.

Obsidian Protocol flips that completely.

---

## 🧠 How Squads Make Decisions

Every squad in Obsidian Protocol constantly evaluates its environment using a layered decision system built from:

- **Utility scoring**  
- **Behavior trees**  
- **Intent interpretation**  
- **Contextual decision‑making**

This means squads aren’t reacting randomly — they’re making informed tactical choices based on the battlefield around them.

Here’s what they consider:

- Threat vectors  
- Terrain elevation  
- Cover availability  
- Visibility cones  
- Suppression zones  
- Enemy formations  
- Intent zones  
- Objective priority  
- Escape routes  
- Breach opportunities  

This evaluation happens continuously, allowing squads to adapt in real time.

---

## 🎯 What This Looks Like in Gameplay

A few examples of autonomous behavior you’ll see:

- A squad under heavy fire retreats behind cover  
- A flanking team pushes through a side alley without being told  
- A breach team identifies a weak wall and plants charges  
- A support squad sets up a suppression line to protect allies  
- Enemy AI coordinates a multi‑angle assault based on your defenses  
- Units dynamically reposition based on visibility and elevation  

No micromanagement.  
No babysitting.  
Just intelligent, emergent combat.

---

## 🔧 Why This Matters

This system is the beating heart of Obsidian Protocol.

It enables:

- Cinematic battles  
- Reactive engagements  
- Tactical depth without micro  
- Smarter AI opponents  
- Larger, more complex encounters  
- A foundation for future systems like commander personalities and campaign AI

Autonomy isn’t a feature — it’s the identity of the game.
