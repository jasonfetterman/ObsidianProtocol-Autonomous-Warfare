Blind Zone (Dense Terrain / Sensor Denial & Information Warfare)

Executive Summary

Blind Zone is a dense‑terrain electronic‑warfare battlefield built for sensor denial, information manipulation, autonomous counter‑recon behavior, and close‑range tactical deception. The environment features tangled vegetation, jagged rock formations, narrow ravines, abandoned bunkers, buried cabling, fog pockets, and natural EM‑disruptive minerals. The map emphasizes Obsidian Protocol’s autonomous sensor‑degraded routing, EW‑aware decision‑making, comms‑relay warfare, and logistics across terrain specifically engineered to break line ‑of‑sight and scramble sensors. In AR (Nreal Light/Air), the map anchors at ~4–5 m viewing distance, covering a 10×10 m physical area representing ~1 km² of dense, sensor‑hostile terrain. Key features include blackout pockets, deception corridors, false ‑signal zones, and information‑warfare‑driven engagements.

Design Goals & Gameplay

Sensor Denial Warfare

Blind Zone prioritizes jamming, occlusion, interference, false signatures, and sensor blackout pockets. Units operate under partial or total sensor loss.

Information Manipulation & Deception

Players deploy EW drones, false‑signal emitters, spoof beacons, and decoy units. Destroying or capturing relay nodes reshapes the entire tactical landscape.
Autonomous Counter‑EW Routing

Autonomous units choose routes based on signal strength, interference pockets, terrain occlusion, and fallback relay availability. Units adapt to misleading data and prioritize verification.
Dense‑Terrain Combat

Close‑range engagements dominate. Vegetation, rock formations, and fog create natural stealth zones and unpredictable movement corridors.

Logistics & Resources

Fuel caches, bunker stations, and buried cable hubs serve as resupply nodes. Convoys must navigate blackout corridors and avoid EW‑driven ambush zones.

Deployment & Progress

A fixed deployment budget (~10,000 points) forces players to choose between EW dominance, stealth infiltration, or sensor‑resistant armor. Blue deploys from the southwest ravine gate; Red deploys from the northeast bunker‑spire command.

Spatial Constraints (Nreal AR)

Device Capabilities

Nreal Light/Air: ~53° diagonal FOV, 1920×1080 per‑eye resolution. Optimal viewing distance ~4–5 m.

Playable Area & Scaling

10×10 m physical area ≈ 1 km² dense‑terrain zone (1:100 scale). Designed for tabletop AR.

Tracking & Occlusion

Inside‑out tracking detects horizontal planes. Depth‑mesh (if available) provides limited occlusion. Virtual objects render over real‑world view; assume minimal real‑world occlusion.

Cable Hub Cluster — (250,800), 120×120 m Buried fiber lines, generators, interior flanking routes.

Ridge‑Shadow Network — (800,300), 200×200 m High‑ground overwatch, signal triangulation, counter‑EW vantage points.

Relay Node B — (150,650), +260 m Comms relay hub and EW‑control node.
