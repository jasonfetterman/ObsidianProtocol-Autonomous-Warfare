⚫ OBSIDIAN PROTOCOL: AUTONOMOUS WARFARE

COMMAND INTENT. UNLEASH AUTONOMY. WITNESS WAR EVOLVE.

Obsidian Protocol: Autonomous Warfare is a next-generation multiplayer military strategy and warfare simulation built around a simple idea:
You don't command every move. You command the war.
Build and maintain your own military force. Deploy aircraft, ground vehicles, naval units, reconnaissance systems, command platforms, and experimental technology. Establish objectives, priorities, rules of engagement, formations, doctrines, and operational intent.
Then let your military execute.
Your forces are not puppets waiting for individual orders. Every unit operates according to its capabilities, sensors, communications, role, doctrine, current orders, battlefield conditions, and understanding of the situation.
You give the intent.
Your military decides how to achieve it.

A DIFFERENT KIND OF RTS

Traditional real-time strategy games ask the player to constantly tell individual units what to do.
Obsidian Protocol takes a different approach.
Instead of spending an entire battle clicking from unit to unit, you operate as a commander.
You establish the mission.
You define the objective.
You determine the priorities.
You establish the rules of engagement.
You decide how aggressive your forces should be.
You determine what risks they are allowed to take.
Then your autonomous military interprets those decisions and attempts to accomplish them.
A reconnaissance unit may detect an enemy formation and investigate.
A commander may redirect nearby forces.
A support unit may reposition to maintain coverage.
A damaged unit may withdraw.
A formation may attempt to flank.
A force may stop advancing because communications have been lost.
A commander may make a decision based on incomplete or outdated information.
The battlefield does not wait for you to manually solve every problem.
It evolves.

🧠 COMMAND INTENT

The foundation of Obsidian Protocol is Command Intent.
Instead of issuing thousands of individual movement and attack commands, you communicate what you want your forces to accomplish.
Examples include:
Attack
Defend
Recon
Support
Escort
Retreat
Hold
Pursue
Flank
Suppress
Breach
Reinforce
Recover
Intent can also contain priorities and operational constraints.
You might order a force to:
Secure the northern approach. Avoid unnecessary losses. Maintain reconnaissance coverage. Withdraw if communications are lost.
The units beneath that command structure interpret the intent according to their own capabilities and circumstances.
This creates a military that behaves like a military rather than a collection of individually controlled game pieces.

🤖 AUTONOMOUS WARFARE

Every unit exists inside a larger autonomous system.
Units evaluate their environment, available information, mission objectives, threats, resources, mobility, damage state, communications, and command intent.
Their behavior can change as the battlefield changes.
A unit that was advancing confidently may suddenly become cautious after losing sensor coverage.
A commander that loses contact with its superior may continue its last known mission, alter its behavior according to doctrine, or enter a degraded operational state.
A reconnaissance element may discover something the main force does not know exists.
A damaged vehicle may withdraw instead of blindly continuing the attack.
A force operating without reliable information may make the wrong decision.
Intelligent behavior emerges from the systems interacting with one another.

👁️ INFORMATION IS POWER

You never automatically know everything happening on the battlefield.
Your military must discover information.
Sensors matter.
Detection matters.
Identification matters.
Communications matter.
Terrain matters.
Weather matters.
Time of day matters.
And uncertainty matters.
Your forces may detect something without knowing exactly what it is.
They may classify a contact incorrectly.
They may know where an enemy was several seconds ago without knowing where it is now.
They may receive information from another unit that is already outdated.
They may lose access to information entirely.
This creates a battlefield where knowledge itself becomes a strategic resource.

📡 THE BATTLEFIELD'S NERVOUS SYSTEM

Military power depends on communication.
Units communicate with commanders.
Commanders communicate with subordinate forces.
Reconnaissance units distribute information.
Relay systems extend communication networks.
Data links allow battlefield intelligence to move between units.
But communication is not guaranteed.
Distance can matter.
Terrain can matter.
Interference can matter.
Damage can matter.
Signal degradation can matter.
A force operating with a strong communications network can coordinate effectively.
A force operating under degraded communications may become fragmented, slow, or uncertain.
This means destroying an enemy's ability to understand the battlefield can be just as important as destroying its units.

🌎 A LIVING WORLD

The battlefield is affected by more than unit statistics.
Time changes.
Day becomes night.
Weather changes.
Visibility changes.
Terrain influences movement and detection.
Environmental conditions influence sensors and operations.
The same military can behave differently depending on the conditions in which it fights.
A reconnaissance operation that works perfectly during daylight may become dramatically more difficult at night.
A communication network that works across open terrain may struggle through difficult environments.
The battlefield itself becomes part of the tactical problem.

🚚 WAR REQUIRES LOGISTICS

A military cannot operate indefinitely without support.
Units require resources.
Operations require preparation.
Damaged equipment requires repair.
Manufacturing requires materials.
Deployment requires planning.
Sustained warfare creates logistical pressure.
A commander who wins every engagement but destroys their ability to maintain the force may still lose the war.
Obsidian Protocol therefore treats logistics as part of military strategy rather than an afterthought.

💰 DEPLOYMENT IS A STRATEGIC DECISION

Owning a powerful military does not mean deploying all of it.
Multiplayer battles operate around a Deployment Budget.
For example:
10,000 Deployment Points
You may own dozens or hundreds of units, but the battlefield imposes a controlled deployment limit.
This creates an important distinction:
Ownership ≠ Battlefield Power
Players can build large persistent militaries without allowing unlimited spending to determine competitive outcomes.
Your deployment becomes a strategic decision.
Do you bring expensive heavy units?
More reconnaissance?
Air support?
Electronic warfare?
Logistics?
A balanced force?
A specialized force?
Every deployment is a decision.
