The Yard (Rail / Industrial / Logistics, Infrastructure, Armored Warfare)

Executive Summary

The Yard is a rail‑industrial battlefield built for logistics warfare, armored maneuvering, infrastructure control, and autonomous force coordination. The terrain features rail spurs, cargo yards, switching stations, warehouses, fuel depots, loading cranes, elevated service roads, and industrial choke corridors. The map emphasizes Obsidian Protocol’s autonomous logistics logic, sensor‑network routing, comms‑relay warfare, and armored operations under dense industrial constraints. In AR (Nreal Light/Air), the map anchors at ~4–5 m viewing distance, covering a 10×10 m physical area representing ~1 km² of rail‑industrial terrain. Key features include multi‑track maneuver zones, warehouse interior combat, rail‑line chokepoints, and strategic control of cargo infrastructure.

Design Goals & Gameplay

Logistics & Infrastructure Warfare

The Yard prioritizes control of rail lines, cargo hubs, switching stations, and fuel depots. Logistics dominance directly affects reinforcement and resupply speed.
Armored Industrial Combat

Long industrial fire lanes favor armor; tight warehouse interiors favor infantry and autonomous squads. Rail cars create dynamic cover and movement obstacles.
Autonomy & Route‑Selection Logic
Autonomous units choose between rail‑side paths, cargo lanes, warehouse interiors, elevated service roads, or off‑road industrial bypasses. Units adapt to blocked tracks and shifting logistics priorities.
Sensor & Information Warfare

Smoke, dust, heat plumes, and industrial clutter distort sensors. Relay towers on cranes and rooftops restore clarity. Destroying relays creates blind pockets across the yard.
Logistics & Resources
Fuel depots, switching stations, and cargo terminals serve as resupply nodes. Convoys must navigate exposed rail corridors and avoid ambush‑prone warehouse zones.
Deployment & Progress

A fixed deployment budget (~10,000 points) forces players to choose between armored pushes, logistics disruption units, or autonomous infantry. Blue deploys from the southwest switching station; Red deploys from the northeast cargo command tower.

Spatial Constraints (Nreal AR)

Device Capabilities
Nreal Light/Air: ~53° diagonal FOV, 1920×1080 per‑eye resolution. Optimal viewing distance ~4–5 m.
Playable Area & Scaling

10×10 m physical area ≈ 1 km² industrial zone (1:100 scale). Designed for tabletop AR.
Tracking & Occlusion
Inside‑out tracking detects horizontal planes. Depth‑mesh (if available) provides limited occlusion. Virtual objects render over real‑world view; assume minimal real‑world occlusion.
